The board

Three rules decide every fight

Standing still is income

A piece that stays gains its site's production. A piece that moves gains nothing that turn, and strength above 255 on one site is lost.

Overkill

Every piece deals its full strength to each enemy on its site and the four next to it, all at once. A 100 between three enemies deals up to 300.

The front is no-man's land

A killed piece leaves an empty neutral site. In a 4-player replay, none of 15,065 ownership changes went straight from one player to another. Fights happen in the gap.

One turn inside ApexBeta

Propose, find the front, predict, search

Propose

Run the full ApexAlpha policy: gathering plans, the value field that routes strength to the border, walls left standing, defence near enemies.

every piece gets a default move

Find the front

Pick our pieces within 3 steps of an enemy. Two pieces can only touch after this turn if they start at most 3 steps apart.

APEX_ZONE = 3
3

Predict

Run the same policy from each nearby enemy's seat: assume the enemy plays like us. Blend it with a second guess, that every enemy stays put.

60% "plays like us" · 40% "stays"
4

Search

Strongest piece first, try all five moves against everyone else's current move, simulate the turn exactly, keep the best. Repeat up to 3 passes.

leaving the default costs a margin of 1
The score of a move set, per scenario
+ enemy strength destroyed − our strength lost − 0.3 × strength spent on neutral sites − strength past the 255 cap
+ 2 × (production + 0.5) for each site we hold − the same for each site the enemy holds

The simulation follows the engine's rules exactly: moves, merges, the cap, overkill, neutral damage and who owns each site afterwards. Only the sites next to a changed piece are rescored, so a full search costs a few milliseconds even on a 50 by 50 map.

What the experiments said

Prediction carries the whole idea

Each line is a 200-game 1v1 match of ApexBeta with one setting changed, against the released ApexBeta. Half means no difference.

Most other settings were flat. The one surprise is depth 2, where enemies are predicted with the policy plus their own search. It beats the released bot 83% of the time because it models that bot exactly, but it does worse against PredatorBot. That is exploitation of a known opponent, so the release stays at depth 1.

Fair play

It never knows who it is playing

ApexBeta never reads opponents' names or code. Its only model of an enemy is its own policy. That policy is ApexAlpha's code, so wins against ApexAlpha are inflated, and the honest yardstick is PredatorBot and ImprovedBot.

Learn the enemy during the game

Each turn ApexBeta checks whether enemy pieces near it moved or stayed as predicted. The next step is to weight its guesses per opponent by that record.
